Crumb Near Me

A bilingual AI dessert guide for Shanghai

RAG Qwen API Agentic AI LLM Evaluation Supabase

Crumb Near Me is a bilingual (中文/English) app that helps people find and keep track of dessert spots across Shanghai — chocolate, cakes, gelato, Chinese sweet soups, bubble tea, and more. Its chat agent, built on the Qwen API (qwen-plus), retrieves real facts from a knowledge base before responding to a photo or typed question — a lightweight RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) pattern — then answers based only on those verified details and gives real metro/bus directions, replying in whichever language you write in rather than guessing when it doesn't actually know.

Evaluation is a core part of this project. It's meant to span all three of the agent's output types — text, vision, and audio — though the deepest coverage so far is on text: 47 weighted questions scored by an LLM-judge, covering single-turn checks (from adversarial prompts that try to hijack the agent's instructions, to questions designed to induce hallucination under pressure) and multi-turn conversations. One test design plants a fact that exists only in the knowledge base, then checks whether the agent actually retrieves it rather than guessing something plausible-sounding — general enough that it could be templated into an auto-generated grounding check for other RAG systems, not just this one. Multi-turn tests matter on their own: they're the only way to catch whether the agent actually carries context across a conversation instead of treating every message as if it had no history, a gap single-turn tests structurally can't expose. Vision has its own 6-case eval for photo identification; audio evaluation is the natural next step.

Beyond the chat guide, visitors can keep a personal dessert log of what they've tried — with photos, ratings, and notes — plus a separate wishlist for places they still want to visit, and get a short taste-summary auto-generated from their own logged entries. They can also leave community notes on a place: freeform tips like a closure or a menu change. These stay explicitly separate from the curated knowledge base, and the agent discloses them as unverified rather than treating them as grounded fact. Accounts run on Supabase Auth, with a guest mode for anyone who doesn't want to sign up.
